ODIA POETRY JHULA · A STORY IN CLOTH

Some things carry
more than words.

A familiar book. An unfinished poem.
A little of home, wherever we go.

A cloth shoulder bag for books, notes and the small things we carry. Inspired by Odisha’s colours and craft traditions, made for readers, writers and everyday wanderers.

THE STORY

Two readers.
A thousand quiet journeys.

The day begins before either of them has found the right words.

01 / MORNING

A page goes with her.

She slips a book into her Jhula, beside the notebook where a poem is still becoming. Across town, he pauses over a line he remembers from home, then puts his pen away. The wooden button closes.

The morning opens.

02 / ON THE WAY

A little of home travels.

Her sari brushes the cloth at her side. His Jhula rests against an everyday kurta. Between errands and conversations, each finds a moment to read, a sentence to keep. Somewhere beyond Odisha, someone opens an Odia book—and a familiar voice returns.

03 / EVENING

A poem finds company.

By evening, they share a table. She reads a few lines. He listens, then offers a page of his own. A bookmark changes books. A thought finds its way into the margin. The bags rest beside their chairs.

There is more to carry home.

Let the cloth carry the book.
Let the book carry a voice.
Let the voice find someone listening.

We imagine the Jhula as part of a poet’s everyday attire: soft cloth, an unhurried silhouette, room for a book and a thought. A sari, a kurta, whatever feels like you. Every design belongs to whoever chooses it.

Rooted in Odisha’s textile vocabulary, this collection hopes to connect readers, writers and local makers. Paid craft work, writer visibility and creator income are the purpose; materials, partnerships and payment terms will need to be agreed before production.

THE POETRY KIT

One thread.
Six companions.

The same border finds another page.
The same colour keeps a pen company.

The proposed Poetry Kit brings together a Jhula, an A5 cloth notebook, a refillable pen, a bookmark, a pen sleeve and an Odia poetry book. The bag’s colour and border carry through its cloth companions.

Eighteen designs are shown with matching kit studies. The two Pattachitra variations show the bag and border only. The poetry book will be selected before production.

EVERYDAY POETIC ATTIRE

A sari. A kurta.
Whatever feels like you.

The same collection belongs to whoever chooses it. Soft cloth and a familiar book can find their place in an ordinary day.

Materials, measurements and craft inspiration

These are contemporary design studies. Tussar, khadi, woven borders, embroidery and painted details describe proposed directions; fabrics and making techniques will be agreed with makers through sampling.

Puri directions draw on Pipili appliqué and Raghurajpur painting. Katak linework translates Tarakasi into proposed embroidery, not silver. Western Odisha studies start with Sambalpur–Bargarh weaving references and do not represent every regional tradition.

The sling target is 90 cm along the exposed strap between its top attachment points, not the vertical drop. Other illustrated dimensions are provisional. Bag-only and kit views are design interpretations; final shape, construction, colourfastness and comfort will be checked on physical samples. Maker partnerships, the poetry-book selection and prices remain to be agreed.
