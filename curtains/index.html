River Rhythm ↗

Design in development

A flowing floral rhythm

Ivory cotton with curving floral medallions, conch-inspired curls and flowering creepers. Lac-red and black borders bring the curtain pair and three pillows together.

The theme, motifs and palette

River Rhythm brings the movement of a flowering riverbank into a room. Large organic medallions alternate with conch-inspired curls and slender creepers, giving the pair an unfolding, asymmetrical rhythm. This is the collection’s design story, rather than a claim that every motif has one fixed traditional meaning.

Palette: Ivory, Lac red, Ink black, Mustard

  • Organic floral medallions lead the ivory field; small sprigs and curls create movement between them.
  • Lac-red floral side lanes sit beside black inner lanes, with mustard accents.
  • A layered floral band, fine rails and a dark finishing band anchor the lower edge.

Bandha is yarn-resist ikat: yarn is tied and dyed before weaving. The soft edges arise from yarn alignment. The proposed design must be developed with an ikat weaver; the digital outline is a composition guide.

Niyamgiri Lines ↗

Provisional - community collaboration pending

Open cloth, measured rhythm

Open natural cotton with proposed geometric bands and diagonal stitch details, paired with natural and forest-green pillows.

The theme, motifs and palette

Niyamgiri Lines is a provisional study in open natural cotton, geometric bands and diagonal rhythms. Its working title evokes the Niyamgiri landscape; the homeware interpretation remains subject to Dongria Kondh collaboration. No named traditional motif, sacred meaning or cultural permission is asserted.

Palette: Natural cotton, Red, Forest green, Yellow, Earth brown

  • Proposed nested-triangle studies and diagonal guards form the upper band.
  • The lower band echoes that rhythm; three diagonal rows add a light transition above it.
  • The main cotton field remains open. Every geometric arrangement shown is provisional.

The proposed approach is Kapdaganda-inspired counted-thread embroidery on cloth, distinct from ikat or supplementary weaving. Motif forms, colour use, placement and suitability for homeware require community review before sampling.

All proposed motifs remain provisional pending Dongria Kondh collaboration and approval.

Regal Borders ↗

Design in development

An ornamental frame for quiet indigo

Midnight indigo, ornamental brick-red borders and matte-gold details. The main botanical tree pillow sits alongside a red buta pillow and an ivory lumbar.

The theme, motifs and palette

Regal Borders treats the curtain as an indigo field held within richly patterned edges. Small floral butas provide a measured rhythm, while the broad lower band adds visual weight. The selected botanical tree pillow gives the coordinated set its principal focal point.

Palette: Midnight indigo, Brick red, Antique gold, Ivory

  • Continuous leaf-and-flower vines sit inside diamond frameworks along the sides.
  • Small floral butas are spaced across the midnight-indigo field.
  • The lower floral lattice is framed by guard bands and fine rails, ending in a plain indigo strip.

The Bomkai direction proposes supplementary weaving: extra-warp side borders and supplementary-weft decoration in the body and lower band. Antique gold describes a matte yarn colour; it is not a claim of metallic yarn.

Aal & Earth ↗

Design in development

Let colour and cloth lead

Quiet earth-red fields, fine contrasting stripes and small stepped forms. Three restrained pillow designs echo the curtain borders.

The theme, motifs and palette

Aal & Earth keeps most of the curtain field quiet so earth-red colour, woven texture and small contrasts can carry the composition. Its restrained stepped forms and fine lines offer a grounded counterpoint to the more ornamental collections.

Palette: Aal red, Earth maroon, Natural cotton, Brown-black

  • A few small triangular forms punctuate the open earth-red field.
  • Narrow side strips and fine contrasting lines frame the pair.
  • A lower row of stepped triangles and fine rails recalls the proposed Phoda Kumbha-inspired direction, pending Kotpad weaver review.

The Kotpad direction proposes Aal-dyed weaving. Dye provenance and woven execution must be developed with Kotpad practitioners. Natural shade variation is part of the intended character, subject to approval of the physical cloth.
