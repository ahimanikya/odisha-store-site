The story behind the collection

01 / BEFORE YOU GO

There is always one more
thing to put in the bag.

A book for the journey. Something to eat. A small gift someone insists you take. At the doorway, a hand rests on your arm for a moment longer than it needs to.

Then come the familiar words: call when you reach.

You leave with your bag a little heavier, and with the feeling of being cared for. That is where this collection begins.

“A keepsake is a small way
of saying: this stayed with me.”

02 / WHAT STAYS

A place is made of
more than landmarks.

It is the first light on water. The stillness of looking up at a temple. The colours of a border you have seen so often they feel like part of your own memory.

It is also the warmth of a family gathering in western Odisha. A meal shared. The joy of a dance. The happiness of being expected somewhere.

These designs bring different parts of Odisha into conversation: the coast and the inland journey, a temple visit and an ordinary morning, the pleasure of travelling and the pull of returning.

03 / A LITTLE HOME, EVERY DAY

The journey continues
in ordinary things.

A cup on your desk. A bookmark halfway through a novel. A bag over your shoulder as you step out into a different city.

None of them can hold a whole place. But they can bring back a feeling, begin a conversation, or become a gift that says, “I thought of you.”

Project Odisha is a collection in the making. These small stories are ideas for objects people can live with, give, and carry.

Materials and craft context

The story above is an original imaginative narrative, not a real family’s testimony. Textile names describe design directions; artisan origin, handloom certification and material provenance have not been established.

Sacred imagery, architectural details and depictions of dress and dance will need knowledgeable local review before production. The bags retain the agreed 90 cm finished exposed sling length, measured along the strap between its top attachments.

Background references: Odisha Tourism, UNESCO: Sun Temple, Konârak, and Sambalpur’s cultural heritage.
